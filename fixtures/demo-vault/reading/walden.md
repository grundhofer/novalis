# Notes on: Walden

Two chapters left, and my opinion has swung twice. The nature writing is beautiful and I
skim it; the accounting is dry and I keep rereading it. There is a literal itemised ledger
of what the cabin cost — boards, nails, hinges — and it's the most modern thing in the book.
He is not arguing for austerity so much as insisting you price things honestly before you
call them necessary.

## Takeaways

- The ledger chapter is the argument. Everything else is decoration on top of it.
- "Cost" is defined as the amount of life exchanged for a thing, which converts every
  purchase into a time budget.
- The book is much less self-sufficient than it advertises, and it's better for admitting
  the visits and the borrowed axe.

## What I'd apply

I want the same ledger for tools. Every app I keep has an ongoing cost — sync failures,
export anxiety, the tax of learning its next redesign — and I've never written those down
next to the subscription price. A one-page tool ledger, priced in hours per year, is the
experiment for September. Connects to [[Plain Text Durability]], [[Data Ownership]] and
[[The Gardener and the Archivist]].

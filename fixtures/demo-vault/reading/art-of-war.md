# Notes on: The Art of War

Read the public-domain translation over three evenings. It is much less about battles than
the title promises — most of the text is logistics, terrain, and the cost of moving people
who would rather stay where they are. The famous lines land differently when you notice how
much of the book is a supply-chain memo.

## Takeaways

- Position is cheaper than force. Most of the winning is done before anything is contested,
  by choosing ground where the expensive fight never has to happen.
- Speed is treated as an economic property, not a heroic one: long campaigns exhaust the
  people financing them, however well they're going.
- Knowing the other side is only half of it; the repeated instruction is to know your own
  state honestly, which is the harder measurement.

## What I'd apply

The supply-chain framing maps almost embarrassingly well onto shipping software. A team can
sustain a sprint of heroics, not a quarter of them, and the failure shows up as attrition
rather than as a missed milestone. Before the next planning round I want a plain estimate of
what a decision costs to *maintain*, not just to make — the same instinct as
[[The Quiet Machine]]. Feeds the cost-of-maintenance argument in
[[The Cost of Proprietary Formats]].

# The Gardener and the Archivist

Raghunathan sets two theories of a personal collection against each other. The archivist
preserves: every item kept in its original state, findable forever, nothing pruned. The
gardener cultivates: notes are rewritten, merged, and killed, and the collection's value is
in what survived rather than in what was captured. Her point is that most people run both
theories at once and then feel bad about the contradiction.

## Takeaways

- The two modes want opposite tooling. Archives want immutability and timestamps; gardens
  want merciless editing and no guilt about deletion.
- Capture volume correlates negatively with retrieval in her interviews — the biggest
  collections were the least used.
- Pruning is the skill nobody teaches, because every tool profits from you keeping more.

## What I'd apply

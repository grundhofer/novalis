# The Cartographer's Objection

Verhoeven spent a decade making nautical charts before writing about models, and it shows.
The book is a patient argument that every map encodes a *purpose*, and that the trouble
starts when a map outlives the question it was drawn to answer — when people keep steering by
a chart made for a different depth of ship.

## Takeaways

- A model is a frozen answer to a question. Write the question down beside it or the model
  will be misread within a year.
- Detail and usefulness diverge past a certain point; his harbour charts got worse when the
  survey data got better, because nobody could find the channel any more.
- Every map has an implied reader. Anonymous diagrams are the ones that mislead.

## What I'd apply

Our architecture diagram is exactly this problem — accurate, dense, and drawn for a question
we stopped asking in March. I want to caption every diagram we keep with the decision it was
made to support, and delete the ones whose decision is settled. Cheaper than maintaining
them, which is the point [[The Quiet Machine]] makes about anything unowned. Feeds the
rewrite-on-contact habit in [[Evergreen Notes]].

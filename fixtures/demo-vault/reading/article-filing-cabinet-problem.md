# Article: The Filing Cabinet Problem

An essay about why every filing system eventually grows a folder called "misc" and what that
folder actually means. The author's argument is that categories are decided at the moment of
filing, when you know the least about how the item will be used, and that the misc folder is
where honest people put the things whose future use they can't yet guess.

## Takeaways

- Filing is a prediction about retrieval. Bad predictions aren't laziness, they're the
  expected error rate of guessing early.
- The misc folder is a healthy signal. Systems that forbid it push the same uncertainty into
  wrong categories, where it's invisible.
- Links beat hierarchy for anything that belongs in two places, which is most things worth
  keeping.

## What I'd apply

I want to keep an explicit inbox rather than pretending everything gets classified on
arrival, and to review it weekly instead of at capture time — by then I usually know what the
note was for. This is the same split [[The Gardener and the Archivist]] proposes, arrived at
from the other direction, and it supports the two-retrieval-modes point in
[[Rooms with Doors]]. See also [[Linking vs Foldering]].

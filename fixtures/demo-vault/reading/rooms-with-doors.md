# Rooms with Doors

A survey of interface metaphors — desktops, folders, canvases, feeds — and which of them
survived contact with thirty years of users. Oyelaran is good on why the folder refuses to
die despite every attempt to replace it with search, and less convincing when she tries to
predict what comes next. The historical two-thirds is worth the whole book.

## Takeaways

- Metaphors succeed when they're *incomplete* in obvious ways. A folder that behaved exactly
  like a cardboard one would be useless; the leaky abstraction is the feature.
- Spatial memory is doing more work than anyone credits. People remember where a thing was
  long after they've forgotten what it was called.
- The search-replaces-structure argument has now failed three times, each time for the same
  reason: search answers the questions you can already phrase.

## What I'd apply

This finally explains why I keep both a folder tree and a tag system and feel guilty about
it. They serve different retrieval modes — location versus description — and neither is
redundant. I'll stop trying to collapse them. See [[Article: The Filing Cabinet Problem]] and
[[Linking vs Foldering]].

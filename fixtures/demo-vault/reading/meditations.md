# Notes on: Meditations

The oldest daily-note practice I know of, and the format is instructive on its own: short
entries, written for nobody, revisiting the same handful of problems without apology for the
repetition. Reading it as a *notebook* rather than as philosophy made it far more useful.
The repetition is the method. He isn't discovering things; he's re-installing them.

## Takeaways

- Writing to yourself removes the performance layer. Nothing here is argued, only reminded.
- The same three or four themes recur for years. A good notebook is allowed to be redundant.
- Attention is treated as the scarce resource, long before anyone was selling it.
- Most entries are a paragraph. Length was never the point.

## What I'd apply

I've been treating my daily notes as a log of what happened, which makes them dull to write
and useless to reread. The shift I want: two lines on what actually occupied my attention,
and one line on what I'd repeat. That's a format I'll keep, unlike the elaborate templates I
abandon every February. Related: [[Notes on: Walden]], [[Evergreen Notes]], and the
repetition argument in [[Spaced Repetition]].

# Zettelkasten and PARA

Two systems that get compared constantly and are not actually competing.

**Zettelkasten** is a thinking method. Atomic notes, one idea each, written in full
sentences, connected by explicit links, with an index that grows from the bottom up. Its
purpose is to produce surprise — connections you did not plan. It optimises for output over
years, which makes it slow and occasionally insufferable in month one. The mature form of
its notes is described in [[Evergreen Notes]].

**PARA** — Projects, Areas, Resources, Archive — is a filing method. Its question is not
"what does this mean?" but "where does this go so I can find it during a busy week?" It
sorts by actionability, and the archive step is the part people skip and then regret.

The synthesis most people land on: PARA for the folders that hold *work*, Zettelkasten for
the links that hold *thinking*. Folders answer "what am I doing", links answer "what do I
know" — the tension mapped out in [[Linking vs Foldering]], and the reason
[[Search vs Browse]] deserves its own note. The archive step everyone skips is what
[[Article: The Filing Cabinet Problem]] is really about.

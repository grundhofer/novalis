# Digital Gardens

A blog is a stream: newest first, finished, performative. A garden is a space: notes at
different stages of growth, edited in place, arranged by topic rather than by date. Nobody
expects a garden to be done.

The convention that makes it work is honest labelling. Marking a note `seedling`,
`budding` or `evergreen` in the frontmatter tells a reader how much weight to put on it, and
tells the author that publishing something half-formed is allowed. Removing the pressure to
be finished is most of the value; the rest is that public notes get corrected by readers.

Gardens fail in two directions. Too private and you are just writing in a folder with extra
CSS. Too polished and you have rebuilt the blog, complete with the paralysis that came with
it.

Practically it is the same vault: [[Evergreen Notes]] as the destination, links rather than
categories per [[Linking vs Foldering]], and plain files so the site generator is replaceable
— see [[File Over App]]. The two-minds version of the same argument is
[[The Gardener and the Archivist]].

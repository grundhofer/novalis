# Linking vs Foldering

A folder is an assertion that a note has exactly one home. That assertion is almost always
false. Is a note on CRDT tombstones filed under algorithms, under sync, or under the project
that made me care? The filesystem forces a choice; a link refuses to.

Links are cheap and additive. Each wikilink is a small, local claim — "these two things
are related" — and the graph that emerges is not designed by anyone. That is the appeal, and
also the failure mode: a hundred notes with no hierarchy is a fog, not a structure. Pure
linkers eventually reinvent hubs and call them maps of content.

The workable arrangement is boring. Folders for *lifecycle* — active work, reference,
archive, per [[Zettelkasten and PARA]]. Links for *meaning*. Tags for *cross-cutting states*
like #reading or #idea that belong to no folder at all.

One practical constraint: whatever structure you choose has to survive being viewed as plain
files on disk, or you have quietly given it up. See [[File Over App]], and the historical case for keeping
both modes in [[Rooms with Doors]] and [[Article: The Filing Cabinet Problem]].

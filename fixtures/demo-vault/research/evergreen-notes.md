# Evergreen Notes

An evergreen note is written to be reread. It states one claim, it is titled like a sentence
rather than a category, and it is edited over years instead of appended to. The opposite is
the meeting-scrap note: dated, contextual, meaningless six months later.

Three habits do most of the work.

**Title as a claim.** "Conflicts should never be resolved by silent deletion" is a note you
can link to and argue with. "Sync notes" is a drawer.

**One idea per note.** Atomicity is what makes linking useful — you can point at the exact
thought instead of a document that vaguely contains it, which is the mechanic behind
[[Zettelkasten and PARA]].

**Rewrite on contact.** When a note comes up again, improve it rather than starting a second
one. Notes get better the way code gets better: by being touched while someone still
remembers why.

Evergreen notes are the natural inhabitants of a [[Digital Gardens]] setup, and they are the
material [[Spaced Repetition]] works on best. Most of mine start as reading notes filed under
[[Reading Index]] and get promoted once they survive a second reading.
